Best pickleball paddles for beginners

Updated July 2026 · 5 picks from hands-on testing

A good beginner paddle does one job: it gets out of your way while you learn the swing. You don't need the most power or the fanciest face. You need something light enough to swing without thinking, forgiving enough that mishits still land, and cheap enough that you aren't nervous every time it hits the court.

I've spent the last few months hitting with eight paddles across every price point. Below are the five I'd put in a new player's hand first, plus one note on what to skip.

What actually matters for a first paddle

  • Swing weight beats total weight. A paddle can read 8 ounces and still feel heavy if the mass sits at the edges. For beginners, lower swing weight means you can react at the net without fighting the paddle.
  • Forgiveness. Widebody shapes and foam-injected edges push the sweet spot wider, so off-center hits stay in play instead of sailing long.
  • Price. You'll outgrow your first paddle. Spend $100 to $200, not $250, unless you already know you'll keep playing.

Our picks

1. Selkirk Amped S2 Widebody

$159.99 MSRP (often ~$100 on sale)

Selkirk · Beginner · Widebody

This is the one I hand to brand-new players. The S2 has the lowest swing weight of anything I tested, so it whips around at the kitchen without tiring your wrist. The widebody face gives you more room to make contact, which matters more than people expect when you're still finding the ball. The fiberglass face is softer than raw carbon, so drops and dinks feel controllable instead of pingy.

2. Bread & Butter Fat Boy Widebody

$189.99

Bread & Butter · Forgiving · Widebody

The Fat Boy is built to be almost impossible to mishit. The widebody face is large, and the aramid weave in the face softens the response so off-center hits don't fly. If a new player struggles with consistency, this is the fix. It runs heavier than the Amped S2, so stronger players will like it more.

3. Selkirk LUXX Control Air Epic

$199.99

Selkirk · Control · Standard (edgeless)

The LUXX teaches placement. Its 19mm core is the thickest I tested, which kills pace and rewards touch. The edgeless design means no edge guard to snag on, and the InfiniGrit surface adds real spin. It costs more than a starter usually should, but if a new player cares about control from day one, this builds good habits.

What to skip as a beginner

The JOOLA Perseus Pro IV tops our all-court list, and I like it a lot. At $249.95 it's still a poor first paddle. A beginner won't feel what makes it good, and that money is better spent on court time. Come back to it after six months of play.
